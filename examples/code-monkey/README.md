# Worked example: "Code Monkey" (Jonathan Coulton)

The repository's proof of concept. Every kind of job the skills do will be shown on this one recording:
- the analysis and the transcription;
- a lead sheet;
- a single part transcribed for a musician;
- an a cappella version;
- band versions in other styles.

## 1. The transcription: the record as a band score
Every part is what the record plays, read from the separated stems and checked against the recording. Nothing is arranged.

**Read these** (`sheets/`, each as PDF and MusicXML, which opens in MuseScore, Sibelius, Finale or Dorico). Each one plays exactly the written-out music; `compact.py` checks it.
| File | What |
|---|---|
| [`sheets/code_monkey_full_score.pdf`](sheets/code_monkey_full_score.pdf) | **the full score**, 125 measures, 16 pages, in playing order (the vocal changes every verse). Staves: lead vocal, backing vocals, electric guitar + TAB, acoustic guitar + TAB, bass + TAB, drums. One-bar repeat signs (%) wherever an instrument repeats its bar |
| [`sheets/code_monkey_lead_sheet.pdf`](sheets/code_monkey_lead_sheet.pdf) | **lead sheet**: melody, words, chord symbols, multi-measure rests (3 pages) |
| [`sheets/code_monkey_drums.pdf`](sheets/code_monkey_drums.pdf) | **drum sheet, short**: ‖: verse 2/3, pre-chorus, chorus :‖ with 1st/2nd endings, % signs, multi-measure rests. 93 printed measures, **2 pages** |
| [`sheets/code_monkey_bass.pdf`](sheets/code_monkey_bass.pdf) | **bass sheet with TAB, short** (same roadmap, 3 pages) |
| [`sheets/code_monkey_guitar.pdf`](sheets/code_monkey_guitar.pdf) | **both guitars with TAB, short** (same roadmap, 8 pages) |
| [`sheets/code_monkey_full_score.m4a`](sheets/code_monkey_full_score.m4a) | **the full score, rendered**: General MIDI mock-up of every part (fluidsynth), 3:18, AAC |
| [`sheets/code_monkey_written_out.pdf`](sheets/code_monkey_written_out.pdf) | **the complete written-out score as first produced**, 125 bars in playing order, errors included (see `evaluation/`) |

The band sheets follow `roadmap_band.py`:
1. Verse 1, Pre-chorus 1, Chorus 1;
2. ‖: Verse 2/3, Pre-chorus 2/3, Chorus 2/3 (first 9 bars);
3. the 1st ending: chorus 2's ending on D, then the interlude, then back to the repeat;
4. the 2nd ending: the last chorus's tags and the outro.

The sources are `song.py` (tune, words and chords, bar by bar), `score.py` (every part), the two roadmaps and `lyrics.txt`.

**Not in git**: building also made 115 AI-generated byproducts, about 660 MB:
- the stems and analysis of the recording;
- the written-out builds;
- MIDI, the MuseScore masters and the 13 audio renders (one per part, the band against the record...);
- the automatic checks, page previews and text groove cards.

[`GENERATED.md`](GENERATED.md) lists each one with its size, checksum and the tool that made it. `regenerate.sh` makes them all again.

Measure numbers are the recording's bar numbers (bar 1 = the first downbeat, 1.07 s), so a doubtful bar can be checked with `look.py` / `partscope.py` at the same number.

**The song**:
- E major, 4/4, quarter = 160. The record runs on a click: 160.01 bpm, with 9 ms of scatter.
- Form:
  - Verse 1 (1-15): no bass; the drums enter with a fill in bar 8;
  - Pre-chorus (16-23): A B G#/B# C#m-B A A B, with a stop on bar 16;
  - Chorus (24-40): B E B/D# C#m E/B-B A A B B, two "Code Monkey like you" tags, then the "you" held over the E turnaround;
  - Verse 2 (41-55); Pre-chorus 2; Chorus 2, which ends on D ("like you... lots");
  - Interlude (80-84); Verse 3 (85-99); Pre-chorus 3; Chorus 3 (108-121);
  - Outro: the last "you" held to the end.

**How each part was read**, with the tools and the decisions:
| Part | How | Decisions to know |
|---|---|---|
| Bar map | the beat tracker, then a fitted grid | The tracker counted 8ths in the drumless intro and invented two 6-beat bars in verse 3. The record follows an exact click, so the bars were refitted to a constant 160.01 bpm grid (residual 9 ms). |
| Lead vocal | pitch tracking (torchcrepe) on the lead stem; on the vocal stem where the double-tracked chorus left the lead stem empty. Rhythm on the 8th grid from the pitch onsets and the word times | Coulton sings low (B2-B3). Scoops are written as their target note. Bar 7 "man-a-ger" slides through G natural, written F#. |
| Words | the fan transcription under the author's Thing a Week post, checked line by line against Whisper | Chorus 2 ends "Code Monkey like you... lots". It is sung D3 falling to B2, and Whisper hears "lots". |
| Backing vocals | the backing stem's pitch compared with the lead's | The chorus is double-tracked: a unison double plus an **octave-up double**, written as the backing part. A three-note chord on "man" (A C# E) is audible but not written. |
| Chords | recorded bass + accompaniment chroma (`look.py chords`) | The verses alternate E and A over an E pedal; verses 2-3 end every 4 bars on A-B. **G#/C** = G# major over B# (the III chord before C#m), written with C so the bass note is readable. |
| Electric guitar | a palm-muted chug that the stem splitter put in the "bass" stem | An E pedal (E3, 8ths) in the verses and the chord roots elsewhere. Written as single notes; play power chords if you like. |
| Acoustic guitar | the "other" stem: 8th-note strums | Written as 8th strums on the chord shapes (TAB). The exact up/down mix in the record isn't readable. |
| Bass | `partscope notes16` (per-16th attacks), recorded-bass NNLS | Bass enters at the end of bar 16. Verses 2-3 play a two-bar figure (E on 1, B on 2&; A on 1, C# on 2&, B on 4). Pre-chorus and chorus are written in quarters with the record's 8th pushes. The tags walk E B A# A / A C# C B. |
| Drums | band-limited onsets of the drum stem, and the spectrum of each hit | Verse: kick 1 and 3, snare 2 and 4, hi-hat on every beat (no 8ths). Pre-chorus: kick 1 3 3&, 8th hats. Chorus: kick 1 1& 3 3&, 8th ride. Stops on 16/56/100; 16th fills on beat 4 in bars 8, 40, 48, 80, 92. |

**How sure**, from `qa.py` at build time (its output, `out/qa.txt`, is listed in `GENERATED.md`):
- **Lead**: 79% of 16ths exact against the recorded singer, 82% the same pitch class. The draft tools reach about 65%. The bars to check by ear are 28, 42, 70, 106, 23, 51 and 107, with 40-60% agreement. Most are double-tracked chorus bars, where the lead stem is thin.
- **Bass**: 83% of 8ths have the recorded bass's pitch class. The differences are in verses 2-3, where the guitar's E3 chug sits in the same stem.
- **Chords**: chroma similarity median 0.81 per bar, with none below 0.6. The lowest are the stop bars (16, 56, 100).
- **Not written**:
  - the chord on "man" in the backing;
  - guitar fills and the exact strum accents;
  - drum ghost notes, and the open/closed hi-hat detail.

## 2. How this was made (proof-of-concept log)
One session of Claude Code driving the skills in this repository (`arranger-core`, plus the band layer of `south-american-arranger` for guitars, bass and drums). The model chose the song, checked the licence, fetched the file, analysed it, transcribed every part, built the scores and wrote this README. The person asked for it in one sentence and commented along the way.

| | |
|---|---|
| Model | Claude Opus 5.5 (`claude-opus-5-5`) in Claude Code 2.1.300 |
| Machine | Linux, GPU NVIDIA GTX 1660 SUPER (6 GB), MuseScore 4.7.4, fluidsynth + FluidR3 GM |
| Date | 2026-10-16 |
| Wall-clock, request to finished files | about 58 minutes (12:10 → 13:09 CEST), including the short sheets and the new `compact.py` tool |
| Model usage for this task | 103 requests: 202k output tokens, 327k input tokens written to the prompt cache, 45.7M cache-read tokens. The cache reads are large because the session already held a long conversation; a fresh session reads far less |
| Price | **about $19** at Opus 5.5 list prices ($4 input, $8 1-hour cache write, $0.20 cache read, $20 output per million tokens): $2.55 to choose and fetch the song, $16.45 for the transcription and sheets. Cache reads are 58% of it, because the session was already long. The evaluation (`evaluation/`) cost $12.54 more |

| Step | Time |
|---|---|
| Choose the song, check the licence, fetch the file | ~10 min (before this task, same session) |
| `analyze.py` (Demucs stems 1:05, karaoke split 2:37, beats 0:10, pitch 0:23, Whisper 2:08, report) | 9:01 |
| Find and fix the bar map (click-exact grid), re-run drums + report | 3 min + 2:02 |
| Read the form, bass, drums, chords, lead, backing (`look.py`, `partscope.py`, pitch on stems) | ~23 min |
| Write `song.py` + `score.py`, fix three build errors (tie syntax, ties across sections, section order) | ~5 min |
| Build full score + 4 sheets (~25 s each), QA | ~3 min |
| Render the audio (13 files) | 3:31 |
| The short sheets (person's request: "use repeat bars"): write `compact.py` from an earlier song-specific script, the roadmaps, fix two layout/playback bugs, build the 5 short sheets | ~10 min |

Mistakes made on the way, and where they are handled now:
- A beat tracker's bars in a drumless intro aren't to be trusted. The bar map was first fitted by hand; `barmap.py --grid --anchor 1.07=1` now does it (160.015 bpm, residual 9 ms, within 2 ms of the hand fit).
- Ties use `E3~:2`, not `E3:2~`.
- A tie that crosses into a new section needs an empty syllable on the continuation note (done in `score.py`; noted in the skill).
- Sections are printed in the order they are created. They were built per section type at first, so the score came out in the wrong order; the file now creates them in bar order and asserts it.
- The first full score was written out bar for bar (16 pages, drums 3 pages). The person reads scores with repeats more easily, and had said so before. The band sheets now use repeats and % signs (drums 2 pages).
- `compact.py`'s first % rule put a repeat sign under a tied note, which broke the tie in playback. Tied bars now stay written; the playback check caught it.

## 3. Checked against the master tracks and fan transcriptions
The sheets above are left exactly as the agent produced them. [`evaluation/`](evaluation/README.md) measures them against two references:
- the song's official unmixed tracks (*JoCo Looks Back* source tracks, CC BY-NC, included in `evaluation/master-tracks/`): lead, double, octave and harmony vocals, five electric guitars, bass and drums;
- four fan chord charts, two tabs and the JoCo wiki lyrics.

The evaluation folder holds the full report, with the errors grouped by cause, and the commands to reproduce every number.

| Part | Against the master track | Main errors |
|---|---|---|
| Lead vocal | 78.9% of 16ths exact, no octave errors | blue notes bent into the key, slides written as their target, notes held through breaths |
| Bass | 89.6% right pitch (pre-chorus 99%, verses 76%) | the chromatic C in the wrong bars; invented E pickups |
| Backing vocals | octave double 84% exact | a whole harmony line (mostly a 5th above) not written |
| Drums | F 88% kick, 90% snare, 76% cymbals | ghost kicks and fill notes left out |
| Guitars | the palm-muted E exactly right | five electric guitars written as one electric plus one acoustic |
| Chords | pre-chorus right | verse riff (E B A, A C#m B) written E, A; Emaj7/D# written B/D#; two A minors written A major |
| Lyrics | 49 of 51 lines | "lots" for "a lot", "To see" for "See" |

The methodological causes are rules in the skill now, in `arranger-core/references/transcription.md` §8.

## Regenerate
`./regenerate.sh` (about 15 minutes with a GPU) makes everything again from `code-monkey.mp3` and the sources:
1. the analysis, then the exact click grid;
2. the written-out builds, the checks, the renders and the text sheets;
3. the short sheets in `sheets/`;
4. the evaluation's scores.

The neural models in the analysis make slightly different stems on every run, so rebuilt sheets can differ in a few notes.

## Layout
```
examples/code-monkey/
  code-monkey.mp3      the recording (CC BY-NC 3.0)
  lyrics.txt           the words (fan transcription, checked against the recording)
  song.py, score.py    the transcription: tune/words/chords; every part
  roadmap_*.py         how the short sheets are laid out (compact.py)
  sheets/              what a musician uses: the five sheets (PDF + MusicXML), the full score rendered (.m4a),
                       the complete written-out score as first produced (PDF)
  evaluation/          the sheets measured against the official master tracks and fan transcriptions;
                       report/index.html is the illustrated report (open it in a browser)
  GENERATED.md         the AI-made byproducts not kept in git (stems, analysis, builds, MIDI, renders)
  regenerate.sh        makes the byproducts and the sheets again
```
